HoodLock vs StonkBrokers.

The honest headline: these two barely overlap. StonkBrokers locks the positions we cannot, and we lock the ones it does not. Which you need is decided by what you are holding, not by which is better.

Updated 2026-07-29 · HoodLock Team
Note

Everything below comes from each platform's own published material as of 2026-07-29, and both sets of contracts are on-chain and readable. Verify before relying on any of it — terms change.

What each one locks#

This is the whole decision, and it is usually settled before price enters into it.

AssetHoodLockStonkBrokers
Plain ERC-20 (creator supply, treasury, team tokens)YesNo
Uniswap v2 style LP tokensYesNo
Uniswap v3 LP position NFTsNoYes
Uniswap v4 native-ETH liquidityNoYes
Token vesting schedulesYesNot offered
Token burning with a recordYesNot offered

HoodLock's locker holds ERC-20 tokens, which is why a v3 position — an NFT — is out of reach for it. StonkBrokers built for exactly that case. If your launchpad graduated you to a v3 pool and handed you the position, they are the ones who can hold it.

The liquidity locker page explains how to tell which kind of position you have, which is worth doing before you choose a platform.

Pricing#

HoodLockStonkBrokers
ModelFlat fee in ETHPercentage of the position, or of collected fees
Cost0.005 ETH per lock, burn or schedule0.5% of locked liquidity at lock time, or 20% of every swap-fee collect
Scales with position sizeNoYes
WithdrawalFree—

The models differ in kind rather than degree. A flat fee costs the same on a large treasury as on a small one; a percentage does not. Conversely, a percentage-of-fees model can be cheaper for a position that never collects much.

Careful

The 20% option is charged on every collect for the life of the lock, not once. On a pool with real volume, that compounds into a materially different number from the 0.5% upfront option. Model both against your expected volume rather than assuming the smaller percentage is cheaper.

Custody and admin#

Both platforms state that no admin key can seize locked liquidity, and both publish contracts you can read. StonkBrokers has been audited by HashLock and publishes the report.

HoodLock's contracts are verified on Blockscout, and the vesting contract was built against a written threat model with a full test suite. We have not had a third-party audit, and we do not describe verification as one — the security model is explicit about the difference and about which functions to read for yourself.

Note

If a third-party audit is a hard requirement for your treasury or your investors, that is a real and legitimate reason to prefer an audited platform for the assets it supports. We would rather say that than pretend the distinction does not exist.

Choosing#

If you hold…Use
Creator, team or treasury tokensHoodLock
A v2-style LP tokenHoodLock
A Uniswap v3 or v4 position NFTStonkBrokers
A vesting schedule for contributorsHoodLock
Both a v3 position and a team allocationBoth — they are not alternatives

That last row is the common case and it is not a diplomatic dodge. A project that graduated to v3 typically has a pool position and creator supply sitting in a wallet, and those are two separate commitments requiring two different tools.

What neither of us can tell you#

A lock on either platform is evidence about one position. It says nothing about the token contract, the distribution of the remaining supply, or the people behind the project. The checks that sit either side of a lock matter at least as much as the lock itself.

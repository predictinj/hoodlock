Using an established locker on Robinhood Chain.

If you have launched before, you probably have a locker you already trust. The question is whether it reaches this chain, and for most of them, today, it does not.

Updated 2026-07-29 · HoodLock Team

The short answer#

Robinhood Chain reached mainnet on 1 July 2026. The large multi-chain lockers have not, as of writing, added it to their published network lists. A locker that does not support a chain cannot lock on it. There is no workaround, because the contracts simply are not deployed there.

Team FinanceEthereum, BNB Chain, Cronos, Fantom, PolygonNot listed
PinkLockEthereum, BNB Chain, Polygon and othersNot listed

Note

This is a snapshot taken on 2026-07-29 from each platform's own published material, and lists change. What does not change quickly is the underlying reason: adding a chain means deploying, verifying, indexing and then supporting it operationally, and a chain that reached mainnet on 1 July is not near the top of anyone's backlog.

Why chain support is not a formality#

A locker is a set of deployed contracts, not a website. Supporting a new chain means deploying, verifying and indexing there, and then supporting it operationally. It is a real decision with real cost, which is why chains that launched four weeks ago are rarely on the list.

It also means you cannot use a locker's reputation from another chain as evidence about this one. What matters is the contract at the address your tokens would go to.

What actually separates lockers#

If you are evaluating where to lock, these are the questions that actually separate lockers, and they apply to us as much as to anyone.

  • Is the contract verified on the chain's explorer? If not, nothing else can be checked.
  • Is there a function that lets an admin move locked tokens? Search the source for withdraw, rescue and sweep, and read who can call each.
  • Can an unlock date be shortened? If it can, the date is a preference rather than a commitment.
  • What is the fee model, and is it capped? A percentage of your tokens and a flat fee in ETH create very different incentives.
  • Can a stranger verify a lock without an account? A lock nobody can check does not do the job you are paying for.

How to choose a token locker works through each of these properly, and our security model answers them for HoodLock with the specific functions to read.

What waiting costs#

Every day spent waiting for a locker to arrive is a day your creator supply sits liquid and visible on the holder list, which is precisely the thing a lock exists to resolve. Buyers reading your token page today cannot see an intention to lock later.

A reputation earned on Ethereum also does not transfer to a contract that does not exist here. What matters is the contract at the address your tokens would go to, and that one you can read yourself in five minutes: the security model names the exact functions and what you should find.

Once it exists, verify the lock — ours included.
